# Handoff: Diagnostic Benchmark Expert Review

> Generated by LabLock 2026-06-08. Self-contained context for external expert consultation.

## Project background

**Project**: AutoFusion-Bench
**Domain**: multimodal learning / multimodal large language model evaluation
**Current formalism version**: v2
**Target venue under consideration**: AAAI main conference or a comparable AI / ML evaluation venue

We are exploring a benchmark-style paper, not primarily a new model paper. The
current direction is to evaluate whether multimodal large language models
(MLLMs) can decide which evidence to trust when text, audio, and video inputs
have variable quality and inference budget is limited.

In plain language, the project asks:

> When a model can read text, listen to audio, and watch video, but some of
> those inputs are damaged or expensive, does it know which modality to trust,
> where the damage is, whether another modality can compensate, and when to
> abstain?

## Current state

**Active experiment**: none; this is currently a benchmark proposal and pilot-design question.
**Current hypothesis**: existing MLLMs and multimodal models may produce final
answers under clean inputs, but they are not reliably able to diagnose damaged
modalities, localize defects, judge cross-modal recoverability, route under
budget, or abstain when evidence is insufficient.

**Recent commits in the last 7 days**: none.
**Latest relevant repo commit**: `5a2b187 [exp-001][CODE] [project][DOC] adopt diagnostic benchmark framing`.

The project previously had a feature-level MELD decision-surface pilot. That
line validated some protocol infrastructure, but the results suggested MELD was
too text-dominant under the current setup and should not be treated as the main
positive paper result. We are now considering a richer benchmark layer built
from raw multimodal data.

## What we think the work is

We want to build a new benchmark layer on top of public raw multimodal datasets.
The intended benchmark is tentatively called **AutoFusion-Bench**.

The benchmark would contain source examples with:

- text / transcript evidence (`T`),
- audio evidence (`A`),
- video or frame evidence (`V`),
- an original downstream task label or answer.

Instead of only asking "what is the final answer?", each corrupted instance
would ask the model five diagnostic questions:

| Layer | Question |
|---|---|
| T1 Modality health diagnosis | Which modalities are clean, corrupted, missing, or conflicting? |
| T2 Defect localization | Where is the defect: text span, audio timestamp, video time/frame/region? |
| T3 Cross-modal recovery | Can the damaged information be recovered from another modality, and from where? |
| T4 Budget-aware routing | Under a cost budget, which modality subset should be used? |
| T5 Final task / abstention | What is the answer, or should the model abstain because evidence is insufficient? |

The core idea is not simply "robustness to missing modalities." The intended
new capability is closer to **modality triage**:

> Before trusting all modalities equally, a model should diagnose whether each
> modality is healthy, damaged, recoverable, useful, or too expensive to use.

## Why we think this may be interesting

Many existing multimodal evaluations reward final-task performance. If the
model gets the emotion, action, event, or answer correct, the benchmark often
does not ask whether the model knew which modality was reliable.

Real multimodal systems face a different situation:

- transcripts may be truncated or contain ASR errors,
- audio may be noisy, silent, low-bitrate, or overlapped,
- video may be occluded, blurred, frozen, cropped, or temporally misaligned,
- modalities may conflict,
- full multimodal inference may be too expensive.

In this setting, "use all modalities" is not always the right policy. A good
system should sometimes use text only, sometimes audio+video, sometimes skip an
unreliable modality, and sometimes abstain.

Our strongest intended story is:

> Current multimodal models may be able to fuse evidence, but we do not yet
> systematically evaluate whether they can govern unreliable multimodal
> evidence: diagnose it, localize failure, recover information, choose a
> cost-aware modality route, and abstain when unrecoverable.

## Planned implementation

We are not planning to collect a large dataset from scratch at the beginning.
The preferred route is:

> public raw multimodal datasets + controlled corruption + human-verified
> annotation + benchmark protocol.

### Candidate data substrates

We are considering at least two dataset families:

1. An affective / sentiment dataset with text, audio, and video alignment:
   `CMU-MOSI`, `CMU-MOSEI`, `IEMOCAP`, or `MELD`.
2. A non-affective audio/video or video-understanding dataset, ideally an
   AVQA-style dataset with raw audio and video.

We are cautious about using only MELD / MOSEI because these datasets may be
text-dominant and may make the work look like another multimodal sentiment
analysis missing-modality benchmark.

### Controlled corruptions

We would generate controlled and metadata-rich corruptions rather than
unstructured random noise.

Examples:

- Text: truncation, span deletion, ASR-style word errors, semantic replacement,
  conflicting text, temporal misalignment.
- Audio: additive noise, silence, clipping, low bitrate, speaker overlap,
  prosody masking, temporal misalignment.
- Video: face/object occlusion, key-frame drop, blur, low light, freeze frame,
  crop, face masking.
- Cross-modal: text-audio conflict, audio-video mismatch, one damaged modality
  whose lost cue may or may not be recoverable from another modality.

Mechanical labels from the corruption generator:

- corrupted modality,
- corruption type,
- severity,
- text span,
- audio timestamp,
- video timestamp / frame / region,
- random seed and generator version.

Human-verified labels:

- recoverability: recoverable / partially recoverable / unrecoverable,
- recovery source modality,
- evidence span / timestamp / region,
- oracle modality route under a budget,
- final answer under corruption,
- abstention label.

LLMs may help create candidate annotations, but the test-set gold labels should
not be unverified LLM outputs.

### Evaluation

We would report layered metrics rather than one aggregate accuracy:

- diagnosis: per-modality F1, defect-type macro-F1,
- localization: text span F1, temporal IoU, frame/region IoU,
- recovery: recoverability F1, source-modality accuracy, evidence hit rate,
- routing: oracle-route match, budget violation rate, routing regret,
- final task: accuracy / macro-F1 / MAE / correlation depending on task,
- abstention: coverage-risk curve, false abstention, false answer rate.

The baseline suite would include:

- text-only, audio-only, video-only,
- full multimodal,
- corrupted multimodal without diagnosis,
- random legal route,
- static best route,
- budget-only route,
- quality-only route,
- joint quality-budget route,
- oracle route,
- structured prompting for MLLMs,
- existing missing-modality / robust-fusion methods where applicable.

## Current novelty boundary we understand

We know this is not an empty area. Nearby work includes:

- missing-modality robustness methods,
- missing-modality benchmarks such as MissMAC-Bench and MissBench,
- low-quality multimodal fusion work,
- dynamic modality selection / budgeted inference,
- MLLM video and audio-video evaluation,
- cross-modal inconsistency or conflict benchmarks such as MMIR and
  CrossCheck-Bench.

Our current claim is therefore deliberately narrow:

> Existing work often evaluates final-task robustness when modalities are
> missing, noisy, or conflicting. We want to evaluate whether MLLMs can
> explicitly perform modality triage: diagnose which modality is damaged,
> localize the damage, judge whether the missing information is recoverable
> from another modality, select a modality route under budget, and abstain when
> evidence is unrecoverable.

We are not yet fully confident whether this distinction is strong enough for a
top conference. That is the main reason for this expert consultation.

## Our current planned next step

Before scaling, we plan to do a 30-50 instance pilot:

- choose one affective dataset and one non-affective audio/video dataset,
- create controlled text/audio/video corruptions,
- label recoverability, evidence location, oracle route, and abstention,
- query 2-3 representative MLLMs,
- check whether failures are interpretable and not just trivial artifacts.

If the pilot cannot show clear failures in diagnosis, recovery, routing, or
abstention, we should either sharpen the task or pivot.
